# "It's just harmless entertainment"

Plenty of people defend a three-hour nightly screen habit by pointing to worse things they could be doing. Staying up late reading costs some sleep, they admit, yet at least they are not drinking heavily or frying their attention span on thirty-second social media clips. Compared to real vices, reading web novels or watching anime looks like harmless entertainment.

A habit does not become harmless simply because worse habits exist. Even without hangovers or empty wallets, spending three hours half-awake in front of a screen still takes a toll on your sleep and mood.

People also give "reading" a free pass just because it involves words on a page. Written fiction sounds wholesome next to short video apps, yet the format matters far less than how you consume it. When you skim thirty chapters of a machine-translated power fantasy past midnight, or let four mediocre seasonal anime episodes play one after another, your brain chases quick novelty in the exact same way a social feed does. Calling it reading simply makes the loop easier to excuse.

## Energy

Pirate manga sites and free video streams charge zero dollars, and lying in bed looking at a screen looks restful on the surface. Because the drain builds gradually rather than causing a single medical emergency, it is easy to assume a free screen habit leaves your daily energy untouched.

Over time, though, daily overstimulation dulls how strongly you feel ordinary daylight energy. Because alertness and patience fade slowly over three or four years, there is no sharp drop to warn you. By the time sluggish afternoons and a short temper settle in, you blame them on getting older or having a busy job. Friends who spend their evenings the same way complain about feeling tired too, so being worn out by noon starts looking normal.

That numbness stays invisible only because it builds slowly. If losing that energy hit all at once over a single weekend instead of stretching across years, you would wonder what illness you caught. Meanwhile, people spend real money on gym memberships, supplements, and strong coffee trying to buy back basic energy while giving it away free to a screen every night. Once you stop draining yourself every evening, ordinary things return quickly: waking up clear-headed and keeping your patience through a normal afternoon.

## Speed-reading

Even after noticing the tiredness, the value illusion usually hangs onto one stubborn line: maybe late nights wear you down a bit, yet you still love experiencing these stories, and enjoying them makes the cost worth it.

Your own habits during an ordinary session tell a very different story. Think about how often you watch anime episodes at 1.5x or double playback speed, tapping forward ten seconds through quieter scenes and hitting Skip Intro before the music even starts. When you read web novels or light novels, your eyes slide diagonally down the page, skipping descriptions and slower dialogue just to catch bold text, a level-up box, or the moment an arrogant rival gets put in his place. Even in visual novels, it is easy to hold down the skip key across whole stretches of slice-of-life conversation purely to reach the next plot twist or romance scene.

Think about how strange that behavior is. Nobody speeds up a favorite song just to get to the final note faster, and nobody skips the middle third of a good movie just to check how it ends. When you genuinely enjoy something, you take your time so it lasts.

The moment you start rushing past slower scenes and speeding up dialogue just to hit the next payoff, your own impatience shows what is really happening. You are simply racing through pages for quick bursts of excitement, and your thumb stopped waiting for a real story hours ago.

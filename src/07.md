# Chapter 7: The Counterfeit Hero: Stolen Agency and Simulated Stats

The protagonist stands at the edge of the abyss, facing an enemy that is vast, ancient, and terrible. He raises the sword and the system window appears in golden text, the stat numbers climbing: *Strength: 847 → 912. Agility: 623 → 701. Level: 147 → 148.* The guild cheers, the love interest gazes with wide, adoring eyes, and the arrogant young master who mocked him in chapter twelve is forced to kneel.

You feel a warm, brief, golden flicker in your chest. It's your pride, your satisfaction, your triumph, for a victory you didn't earn, in a world that doesn't exist, won by a character whose struggles were written to be easily overcome.

The flicker fades within seconds. The next chapter begins, the next enemy appears, the next stat window climbs, and the flicker returns and fades again. You scroll, hunting it and needing it, while it gets thinner, the scrolling gets faster, and the chapters blur. You're strip-mining now, and the Big Monster whispers: *"This is deep. This is meaningful. This is character growth. This is the power system. This is why you read."*

At the end of the last chapter I promised to show you the thing that rides on your love of stories and wears that love as a mask, the thing that whispers that without the next level-up and the next face-slap your life will be flat and gray. It isn't called story, hobby, or appreciation. I call it the Parasite.

## The vine on the garden

I'll be gentle here, because this touches the place where your love of stories lives. A part of you has been listening to tales since before you had words, a screen, or a page. It sat by the fire and heard the myth, opened a book and felt the transport, and looked at ink and saw a world. That part is real and it's yours, older than any screen, and I'm not here to take it. No one could.

But something has wrapped itself around that ancient hunger and begun to feed, and what feeds isn't you, your love, or your wonder. It's the Parasite.

The Parasite is the commercial compulsion loop, the infinite, serialized, algorithmically engineered delivery mechanism. It's the four-hundred-chapter cultivation clone, the copy-paste isekai, the system manhwa with identical stat windows, the romcom whose manufactured misunderstanding stretches across forty chapters, the harem bait, the face-slapping formula, the power-fantasy assembly line. It takes your hunger for story and feeds it sugar water, which gets called content, then the next chapter, then the reason I read. You drink, and the hunger is never resolved, because sugar water isn't food.

The Parasite whispers: *"This is what you love and need. Without it your life will be flat and gray, and you'll have nothing."* You believe it because it speaks in the voice of your own love and wears that love's face. But it isn't that love. Hear this the way you'd hear a friend with a hand on your shoulder: it wasn't you, it was the thing on your back, and the thing is small and can be set down.

Picture a garden you planted and tend: flowers, herbs, vegetables, fruit trees. One day a fast-growing, aggressive vine appears and wraps around everything. The flowers wilt, the herbs yellow, and the fruit trees thin, until you look at the garden and see only the vine and say, *"This is my garden. This is what I love."* But the vine is something growing on the garden. The garden is underneath, still alive and still beautiful, and it will bloom again once the vine is removed. The garden is your love, and the vine is the Parasite.

The garden is *Vagabond* and *Slam Dunk*, the physical book on the Sunday afternoon table that you savor and close with a satisfied sigh, the bounded, completed, nourishing masterwork that gives you something and lets you go. The vine is the four-hundred-chapter cultivation clone and the infinite-scroll webtoon, the serialized treadmill that never resolves and never lets go. You don't need the vine to love the garden, and you never did.

## Sugar water for a starving body

Now the mechanism, so you can see the gears.

You have a genuine, deep, ancient hunger, and it isn't for the flicker, the stat window, or the face-slap. It's for competence and mastery, the feeling of *I built this, I earned this, I'm more capable today than I was yesterday.* It drives the craftsman, the athlete, the musician, and the scholar, and it's legitimate.

The Parasite feeds that hunger a counterfeit: the stat window, the level-up, the cultivation breakthrough, the face-slap, the protagonist's victory. You feel the flicker, your brain registers it as progress, and for a fraction of a second you feel you've done something and advanced. But you haven't built, learned, or grown anything. You've been horizontal and motionless, scrolling. The protagonist's strength isn't yours. Your own project sits untouched on the desk, your body hasn't moved in four hours, and your skills haven't grown. When the flicker fades you're exactly where you started, four hours poorer, more depleted, and further from the competence you hunger for.

A child playing a video game levels up and cheers, "I did it!" The parent says, "Yes, you did," and the child beams. The parent doesn't point out that the character leveled up while the child pressed buttons, because the child is seven, the joy is real, and the parent loves the child. You aren't seven, and what you're feeling is a borrowed flicker. That borrowing is the Counterfeit Substitution, the Parasite's favorite meal. You've been feeding it one flicker at a time, and the flickers are getting thinner while the Parasite gets hungrier and you get emptier. The Big Monster calls this character growth, and you believe it because it speaks in the voice of your own tired, hopeful heart.

The cycle sustains itself like this. The Parasite takes four hours a night, maybe more, that you could have spent building real competence by cooking, walking, learning, resting, and being in your body and the world. Their absence leaves a feeling of stagnation, a flatness, a sense that you aren't growing. The Parasite points to that stagnation: *"See? You need me. Without me you'd feel flat."* You open the app, the flicker fires, the stagnation is masked, and your real hunger for competence goes unfed. The sugar water gets called progress, and progress gets called character growth.

Remember the tight shoes from Chapter 4. The simulated level-up relieves the feeling of inadequacy, but the reading created that inadequacy by stealing your time and agency. The stagnation is the tight shoe and the flicker is its removal. You thank the Parasite for the meal and reach for another while the garden wilts.

The Parasite says your life will be gray without it, and you look around and the world does look gray. But the grayness isn't the absence of the reading. It is the reading. The Parasite steals your time, presence, and vitality, then points at the emptiness as proof that you need it. You can't see the vine because it covers the garden, so the vine gets called life and the garden gets called nothing.

Picture someone who wears dark sunglasses indoors, all day, every day, for months or years. The room looks gray, the food dull, and the faces flat, and they say, "The world is dim." Someone says, "Take off the sunglasses," and they answer, "No, the sunglasses are what let me see." They aren't blind. They're dimmed, and the sunglasses are doing it. The Parasite dims the world and then says, "See? Without me it's dark." The person calls the grayness life, the sunglasses sight, and the Parasite love.

## The garden is still alive

Now the part I most need you to hear. I won't tell you what to do. I only ask you to remember.

Think of the last time you built something: cooked a meal from scratch, walked a long distance, had a conversation that mattered, finished a task, learned a skill. Remember the slow, full satisfaction of *I did this, I earned this.* It wasn't the borrowed flicker. It was the kind that settles in the body and stays, and leaves you more present, more here, and more yourself.

Was it the same golden pulse as the level-up? Or was it slower, deeper, warmer, something that didn't need a screen and didn't leave your eyes burning and your brain foggy? Genuine achievement is a warmth, a settling, a fullness. It needs no stat window, level number, or protagonist's victory. It needs only your own hands, mind, and time. You have hands, a mind, and time, but the time is being stolen, the hands are idle, and the mind is foggy. You say, *"I don't know what else to do."*

You do know. You knew before the Parasite and you'll know after, and the knowing is buried under the vine, the grayness, and the four hours of scrolling. It will return when the vine is cut and the time is reclaimed, and it will feel like the garden blooming, a warmth that is real and enough.

The garden is still there under the vine. The flowers are rooted, the herbs are fragrant, and the fruit trees are bearing, and you can't see them only because the vine covers them. The vine is the four hours, the stolen time the garden needed, and the garden is your actual, singular, unrepeatable life. It waits, patient and without blame, and when the vine is cut it won't hurt. It will feel like the first deep breath after four hours of shallow, screen-lit stillness.

One last thing makes the vine small. The Parasite isn't powerful, and it isn't you. It's a fast-growing, hungry vine, and it needs you, and you don't need it. It needs your attention, your time, your next click, your dry eyes, your foggy brain, and your tomorrow, and without them it starves, quietly and painlessly. It doesn't rage or fight. It thins until it's gone, like a candle flame in a room where someone has opened a window.

The Parasite can't lift your hand, tap the screen, or open the app. It can only whisper, in the Big Monster's voice, which is the 99 percent, and that's a belief, and beliefs can be examined, questioned, and shown to be false. So the Parasite makes sure you never examine the whisper, because once you see the vine it loses its power. It's afraid of the seeing, which it can't survive.

Remember the first chapter: the enemy is the loss of choice, and here is the proof. If the reading were really about deep plot and character growth, about appreciation and the love of narrative craft, you could savor one chapter, close the app, and feel full. You can't stop at one, or ten, or forty. The hand reaches again and the Big Monster says *"This is character growth."* That inability shows the appreciation is compulsion, and the compulsion is the Parasite's grip. The grip releases through seeing, and force won't release it.

You don't need to cut the vine tonight, close the app, or walk away from the story. The Paradoxical Instruction holds, and you're still reading and observing. But tonight, when the protagonist levels up, the stat window appears, the flicker fires, and the Big Monster whispers *"This is character growth,"* add one small question: *Is this the Parasite's meal? Is the flicker the sugar water that keeps me starving?* Plant it, let it sit, and let the seeing do the work.

The Parasite isn't you, your love, or your identity. It's a vine on a garden, and the garden is your love, your wonder, and your ancient hunger for story. The garden is still alive and still yours, and the vine can be cut. Cutting it is a seeing and doesn't require a battle. You don't need the Parasite to love stories, the sugar water to feed the hunger, or the flicker to feel alive. You've also already started, because you're reading this.

The fourth room is walked and the Parasite is named. You're still reading your chapters, and the observation is working. You felt it, didn't you? The aching recognition: *The thing I've been feeding isn't the love. It's the thing eating the love.*

Let it sit. Tomorrow, when the Big Monster whispers *"Real life is too boring. Too demanding. Too gray. Fantasy is where I feel alive,"* hear the Parasite's voice, the vine's whisper, dimming the garden and then saying *"See? Without me it's dark."*

In the next chapter I'll ask you the questions the Big Monster doesn't want you to ask, about reality, the flatness, and the grayness, and whether the fantasy world is really more alive than the one you're sitting in now. I'll let you answer, and your answers may surprise you, because they'll come from the garden and not the vine. The garden has been waiting to speak, and it will feel like sunlight.

# "It helps me unwind"

After a long day of work, classes, or commuting, sitting down on the couch feels earned. That tired moment is when the value illusion offers its favorite excuse: you worked hard today, you are wiped out, and opening a light novel, an anime episode, or a visual novel route is how you switch off and unwind.

Minutes after an opening theme starts or a light novel chapter loads, physical tension drops and restlessness fades. Because that relief arrives right as the screen lights up, the relaxation excuse outlasts almost every other justification.

That sudden calm only tells half the story, though, because releasing tension and restoring energy are two separate processes. To see why, you have to ask where that tension came from before the screen turned it off, and what the session leaves behind once it ends.

## Stress relief

Real tiredness from a tough shift or a pile of assignments needs time to wear off. People who do not rely on nightly binges get home tired, eat dinner, take a shower, talk with family, or sit on the couch doing nothing in particular. They still feel tired, yet without extra stimulation, their energy settles down naturally over the evening.

Someone stuck in a nightly screen loop carries a second layer of discomfort mixed right into that normal tiredness: leftover sleep debt from last night, unfinished cliffhangers hanging around in memory, and a primitive brain conditioned to expect fast stimulation after dark. By nine or ten at night, both layers blend into one restless mood that feels just like job stress.

When you open a web novel or queue up an anime, the story numbs everyday frustration through distraction while temporarily scratching yesterday's leftover dopamine itch. Because that restlessness disappears the instant the story starts, the value illusion hands all the credit to the screen, ignoring that yesterday's binge created half the discomfort in the first place. Meanwhile, real exhaustion stays untouched underneath, waiting until you finally shut the device off with even fewer hours left to sleep.

The exact same trick powers the "I earned this" excuse. The value illusion turns temporary relief into an earned treat, telling you that after a hard day you deserve to binge. Genuine rewards, like a good meal, relaxed time with friends, or a full night of sleep, restore you and leave you better off the next morning. Calling a three-hour binge that steals tomorrow's energy a "well-earned reward" gets the trade completely backwards, especially when you are mostly paying to settle a restlessness yesterday's session created.

## Sleep

Plenty of people ask a fair question here: how can reading a slice-of-life light novel or tapping through a visual novel in a dark bedroom be stressful when your body is lying completely still?

When you lie still in bed, your muscles stop moving while your nervous system stays switched on. Anime, manga, web novels, and visual novels are paced to keep your attention in gear through an upcoming fight, a sudden romance twist, or a dialogue choice waiting two minutes ahead. Stories built to keep you clicking never want your mind to settle down, because if you calmed down on page three, you would fall asleep. Lying there for three hours glued to cliffhangers is like sitting in a parked car inside a garage with the handbrake up, holding down the gas pedal and calling the noise "letting the engine cool down."

That constant revving also explains why many readers swear they cannot sleep without a screen first. When you leave the phone across the room at ten o'clock for the first few nights, you may stare at the ceiling for twenty minutes while your primitive brain waits for its usual late-night rush. If you mistake that brief restlessness for real insomnia, you grab the screen back and keep going until exhaustion knocks you out at two in the morning. Passing out from sheer fatigue leaves you half-rested, so you wake up foggy, lean on coffee to get through the afternoon, come home worn out, and reach straight back for the screen that night to numb the crash. You end up running the whole day on yesterday's exhaust.

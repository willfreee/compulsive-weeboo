# Chapter 18: Walking Out of the Screen: Permanent Freedom

The tab is closed and the screen is dark. The room is quiet, your hands are empty, and your mind is clear. The vine is cut, the tight shoes are off, the fog has lifted, and the earplugs are out. The garden is blooming and the fire is burning.

I know the question that comes in the first hours and days after the closing, the last and quietest one: *But will it last? Will the Parasite come back, the vine grow again, the Craving return, the flicker call me back, the void swallow me again?*

I'd like to answer it one more time, the way a friend would who has already walked the path and is standing in the garden: it will last, because the seeing is done and the seeing is the freedom.

You saw the cardboard furniture, the papier-mâché walls, and the sand foundation. You saw the formula, the flat dialogue, the manufactured cliffhanger, and the hollow center. You saw the flicker fail to nourish you, and you saw the Parasite die. The cathedral is empty, and you can't unsee what you saw, so it can't be rebuilt.

You don't need to maintain the freedom, guard it, fight for it, or stay vigilant. You saw, and that seeing doesn't need defending.

Imagine watching a magician show you the wires, the mirrors, and the trapdoor. You wouldn't have to resist the trick afterward or stay on guard against it. You'd simply not be fooled, and if the magician told you to stay disciplined or the trick would fool you again, you'd smile and walk away.

## The bell goes quiet

The Little Monster won't come back as a wolf, a demon, or a predator in the dark. It's a bell's hum, and the hum is fading.

On the first day you may still notice it. Your hands may feel empty and your mind may reach for the familiar scroll. The reaching isn't a failure. It's the last echo of a body that was trained to reach, and it fades within hours or a day or two, gently and painlessly, as the Little Monster starves.

In the first week the hum is rare, a faint restlessness when your hands are empty, the room is quiet, or your mind is idle. It fades within a breath, so gently you may barely notice. Then it's gone, and the going isn't a loss but a return to your baseline, the natural state, and the quiet of the garden.

If you struck a bell and then stopped, you wouldn't worry about the hum returning, guard against it, or set alarms for it. You'd rest in the silence.

## The not-wanting

Now the mindset, which isn't a system, a plan, a set of rules, a boundary, a limit, a discipline, or a vigilance. It's the natural, effortless state of a person who has seen.

You don't need rules, limits, strength, or a guard against anything. You simply don't want it. You aren't resisting the sugar water, the tight shoes, or the slot machine, because you've seen each of them, and the seeing is the not-wanting.

Your Real Hunger is feeding in the garden, in your focus, sleep, clarity, presence, warmth, love, fire, and tales, and it doesn't need the vine, the Parasite, or the Craving.

You'll still love stories. You'll still sit by the fire and hear the tales, open a book and feel the world, and watch a film through the credits. You'll still love the manga and the anime, the wonder and the transport, and none of that love was ever the vine.

Here is how it will go in practice.

If someone asks what you read, you can say: *I read stories. Bounded, completed ones that give me something and then let me go, that nourish me and then release me. They're the garden, and the garden is mine.*

If a friend sends you a link to the next four-hundred-chapter cultivation clone, you can smile and say: *Thanks, but there's nothing there for me.* That isn't a struggle or a sacrifice. It's the truth.

If the voice whispers *"Just one more,"* you'll hear it and recognize it as the Big Monster, the cardboard furniture of a cathedral that can't be rebuilt, and the voice will fade.

If the world says *"You're missing out,"* you can smile and say: *I'm not missing out. I'm walking in the garden, and the garden is full.*

If the part of you that remembers the vine whispers *"I miss it,"* you'll recognize the Phantom Void, the Parasite's last whisper. The garden isn't empty, and that whisper will fade too.

The fire will stay burning, the tales will stay told, and the transport will stay felt, and all of them belong to the garden.

You're free, and the freedom isn't a loss but a homecoming. The garden has been waiting, patient, without blaming you for the vine.

The freedom is permanent, and strength, vigilance, and fighting have nothing to do with it. You saw, and the seeing can't be unseen. The Little Monster will quietly starve, the Big Monster will stay quiet, the Parasite will stay dead, and the vine will stay cut. The garden will stay yours.

So the book is done. The cathedral is empty, the vine is cut, the fog is lifted, the bell is silent, and the seed has grown.

I'll say one last thing, which has been true since the first page. You aren't a reader of trash, a scroller of infinite chapters, a hunter of stat windows, or a chaser of cliffhangers. You're a lover of stories. You sit by the fire and hear the tales, feel the wonder, and keep the transport, and you tend the garden of the bounded, the completed, the nourishing, and the true.

Go and walk in the garden. Feel the sun, hear the birds, smell the soil, touch the flowers, taste the fruit.

Welcome home.

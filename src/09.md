# The "good art" alibi

Most readers admit fairly quickly that copy-paste web novels are junk. Where the value illusion really digs in its heels is with stories you respect. When you stay up until three in the morning reading *Slam Dunk* or *Vinland Saga*, watching an acclaimed anime, or finishing a brilliant visual novel route, the excuse sounds respectable: this is great art, and staying up late just means you care about quality.

Great art deserves respect, yet staying up until three in the morning to devour it has very little to do with appreciating quality. High literary or visual skill never shuts off the binge switch. Worse, once fatigue kicks in past midnight, a marathon flattens the exact qualities that made a masterpiece worth picking up in the first place.

## Masterpieces

Great authors make you care about what happens next. When Shohoku is down four points in the closing minutes against Sannoh, or when Thorfinn faces a brutal turning point in *Vinland Saga*, the tension feels real. Your primitive brain, however, cannot tell Takehiko Inoue's linework apart from a cheap cultivation cliffhanger. All it registers past midnight is an unresolved fight or mystery, and all it wants is the outcome right now.

Look at what happens to your reading pace at two in the morning. Inoue spent years putting deliberate silence into *Slam Dunk*: a player catching his breath, a close-up of tired eyes, a quiet exchange between teammates before the final shot. Makoto Yukimura built whole chapters of *Vinland Saga* around slow conversations meant to linger. When you speed through four volumes half-asleep just to see who wins the match or survives the duel, you barely look at a single drawing for more than a second. You skip the quiet beats just to get to the scoreboard.

The same thing happens when you stack six heavy anime episodes in one night, blast through two dense light novel volumes before dawn, or click straight through an eight-hour stretch of a top visual novel. Tired working memory cannot hold that much weight at once. Major turning points blur together, subtle details pass straight over your head, and by morning you remember the plot summary while forgetting what made the story moving. You can tell yourself you were admiring great art, yet in practice you treated a masterpiece like a basic plot summary just to get to the ending.

## Stopping mid-arc

Once you recognize that marathon pacing ruins the story, the value illusion tries two practical threats to keep the tab open. First it claims that stopping right before a climax breaks the emotional momentum and spoils the scene. Next it warns that even if you put the volume down now, you will lie awake staring at the ceiling thinking about the next chapter anyway, so you might as well push through until it finishes.

Neither claim holds up once you test it. Good suspense does not fade just because you went to sleep. When you pause *Slam Dunk* or stop after one intense anime episode at eleven o'clock, the excitement stays with you until tomorrow, and your mind gets room to absorb what happened. Returning the next day with fresh eyes makes the payoff land far better than pushing through chapter thirty when you can barely focus. Tiredness is what kills a great climax, turning real excitement into dull impatience to reach the last page.

As for lying awake thinking about what happens next, that restless buzz lasts roughly ten or fifteen minutes once the device stays out of reach. Your primitive brain is simply protesting an unfinished hook. Treating fifteen minutes of curiosity like full-blown insomnia costs you four hours of sleep and wrecks the next day simply because you refused to wait until tomorrow.

Respecting a masterpiece means giving it your attention while you are awake enough to take it in, and closing the cover before fatigue hands control back to your primitive brain.
